However, a full platform SDK (like the Android SDK) contains much more than just a network client—it includes emulators, build toolchains, and local system libraries
			
ML
A subfield of artificial intelligence (AI) that gives computers the ability to learn without explicitly being programmed; the development and study of statistical algorithms that can learn from data and generalize to unseen data, and thus perform tasks without explicit instructions.


LLM
An artificial intelligence (AI) system that can understand, process, and generate human language. LLMs are trained on large amounts of data, such as books and articles, using machine learning techniques.


NMT
A method of automated translation that uses artificial neural networks to translate text from one language to another, essentially mimicking the human translation process by learning patterns from large datasets to predict the most likely sequence of words in the target language; a data-driven approach to machine translation considering the entire sentence context rather than just individual words.


GPT
A type of artificial intelligence (AI) model that can create human-like text. GPT is a family of neural network models that use a transformer architecture. 


MIME
Many file types can be translated by Google Translate API (NMT model). Translation will keep the original format.

EO Data
Earth Observation (EO) data refers to information collected about the Earth's physical, chemical, and biological systems using remote sensing technologies such as satellites, airborne sensors, and ground-based instruments


NVDI
a simple remote-sensing metric used to measure and monitor the health, density, and growth of live green veg

NDVI Value Scale
• -1.0 to 0: Water bodies, snow, ice, or clouds (negative values typically represent water).
• 0 to 0.1: Barren rock, sand, or urban/built-up areas with little to no vegetation.
• 0.1 to 0.3: Sparse vegetation, such as grasslands, shrubs, or early-stage crops.
• 0.3 to 0.6: Moderate vegetation, such as temperate forests or stressed crops.
• 0.6 to 0.9: Dense, healthy, thriving crops or forests.
• 0.9 to 1.0: Extremely dense vegetation, such as tropical rainforests (though rarely hit a full 1.0)

• Light absorption and reflection: Healthy plants contain chlorophyll, which absorbs visible red light for photosynthesis and strongly reflects near-infrared (NIR) light.  Stressed, dying, or sparse plants reflect less NIR light and absorb more red light.

• The Formula: It is calculated using satellite or drone sensor data with the equation:
\(\text{NDVI}=\frac{\text{NIR}-\text{Red}}{\text{NIR}+\text{Red}}\)


USGS EROS
Earth Resources Observation and Science (EROS) Center located in Sioux Falls, South Dakota, is a federal government facility and a global leader in land imaging, remote sensing, and terrestrial change research. Initiated in 1966 and built on decades of historical data, EROS acts as the primary guardian of the world’s largest civilian collection of images of the Earth's surface.

GEE
Google Earth Engine is now at the forefront of sustainable natural resource management, climate and disaster risk resilience, and sustainable sourcing of commodities to meet zero-deforestation commitments. In addition, new startups are now applying GEE technology to transform science and EO data into innovative sustainability-focused ideas and enterprises.


 GEE community
 Google Earth Engine (GEE) community is a highly active global ecosystem of remote sensing scientists, GIS professionals, and developers. Because Google Earth Engine hosts the entire historical Landsat and NASA archive natively in the cloud, the community acts as a vital collaborative force for sharing scripts, open-source datasets, and troubleshooting analysis methods. The "Awesome GEE Community Catalog" (https://gee-community-catalog.org/) is a massive community-driven data commons that supplements Earth Engine’s official data repository ==> Community Catalog Tutorials

Dynamic World
a groundbreaking 10-meter near real-time (NRT) global land use and land cover (LULC) dataset produced through a partnership between Google and the World Resources Institute (WRI). By processing European Space Agency (ESA) Sentinel-2 satellite imagery through deep learning models hosted directly on Google Earth Engine, it generates daily land cover predictions for the entire planet. Unlike traditional, static land cover maps that are published annually or once every few years, Dynamic World updates continuously. A new map is created as soon as a fresh, relatively cloud-free Sentinel-2 image is acquired—often within just two to five days of the satellite flying overhead

NAIP
The National Agriculture Imagery Program (NAIP) is a US government program to acquire imagery over the continental USA using airborne sensors. Data are collected for each state approximately every three years. The imagery has a spatial resolution of 0.5–2 m, depending on the state and the date collected.

MODIS
Moderate Resolution Imaging Spectroradiometer
	• Platforms: Terra (launched 1999, morning overpass ~10:30 AM) and Aqua (launched 2002, afternoon overpass ~1:30 PM).
	• Status: Nearing the end of operational life and orbital drift due to fuel limits, with final calibration/reprocessing underway and operations ending around late 2026/early 2027.
	• Capabilities: 36 spectral bands with resolutions of 250m, 500m, and 1000m

VIIRS 
Visible Infrared Imaging Radiometer Suite
	• Spatial Resolution & Sensitivity: VIIRS 375m thermal bands offer higher sensitivity for detecting smaller fires and feature crisper pixels across wider swaths than MODIS 1000m thermal bands.
	• Orbit Coverage: MODIS provided both morning (Terra) and afternoon (Aqua) local crossing times. VIIRS primarily covers afternoon orbits, with morning sampling supplemented by partners like Sentinel-3.
	• Data Access: Users are directed to the NASA LAADS DAAC MODIS-to-VIIRS Transition Portal for continuity products, common algorithms, and migration support

EnMAP and PRISMA
EnMAP (developed by the German Aerospace Center, DLR) and PRISMA (developed by the Italian Space Agency, ASI) are the twin pillars of European spaceborne hyperspectral remote sensing. While most traditional Earth-observation satellites look at the planet in only a handful of broad color bands (multispectral), these two missions use imaging spectroscopy to split reflected light into hundreds of narrow, continuous channels.

	• EnMAP: Heavily optimized for global vegetation, soil chemistry, agriculture, forestry management, and inland/coastal water quality.
	• PRISMA: Excellent for resource management, land-use studies, mineral mapping, hazard response, and point-source methane leak detection

Both missions have outlived their original nominal lifespans and are operating on extended missions through at least late 2027. They are actively serving as critical bridge data to validate upcoming next-generation hyperspectral constellations, such as the European Space Agency's (ESA) highly anticipated CHIME mission and ASI's own PRISMA Second Generation (PRISMA2GEN) constellation

EMIT (NASA, on ISS)
Earth Surface Mineral Dust Source Investigation 
	• cutting-edge hyperspectral instrument
	• excels at identifying point-source greenhouse gases, capturing distinct methane (CH₄) and carbon dioxide (CO₂) plume signatures from landfills, agricultural operations, and energy infrastructure.
	• data is used to track vegetation health, harmful algal blooms, and wildfire characteristics

DESIS (DLR, on ISS)
Earth Sensing Imaging Spectrometer
	• cutting-edge hyperspectral instrument
	• Optimization for coastal and inland water quality, precision agriculture, forestry management, and rapid humanitarian disaster mapping (wildfires, floods, oil spills).


Planet Labs
PlanetScope and SkySat are the foundational commercial constellations operated by Planet Labs. Together, they pioneer a "tip-and-cue" monitoring workflow, where the massive PlanetScope fleet broadly flags daily changes across the globe, prompting the targeted SkySat fleet to zoom in on high-interest areas with sub-meter clarity.

Local Moran’s I
a powerful spatial autocorrelation metric that serves as an effective indicator of spectral heterogeneity in remote sensing and geospatial analysis. While global spatial metrics provide a single summary value for an entire landscape, Local Indicators of Spatial Association (LISA)—such as Local Moran’s I—evaluate spatial variability at the pixel or neighborhood level.

SVH
Spectral Variation Hypothesis, high spectral heterogeneity correlates strongly with high species richness and structural habitat diversity.
