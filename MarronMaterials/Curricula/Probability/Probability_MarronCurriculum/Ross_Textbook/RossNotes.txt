Student in Kolmogorov set theoretic probability. Given the events E, F, and G provide set theoretic expressions for the following:
a) only F occurs
b) both E and F occur but not G
c) at least one event occurs
d) at least two events occur
e) all three events occur
f) no event occurs
g) at most one event occurs
h) at most two events occur

a) Only F occurs
This means:
    F occurs
    E does not occur
    G does not occur

Set-theoretic expression:
	F ∩ Ec ∩ Gc


b) Both E and F occur, but not G
This means:
    E and F occur
    G does not occur

Set-theoretic expression:
	E ∩ F ∩ Gc


c) At least one event occurs
This is the union of all three events.

Set-theoretic expression:
	E ∪ F ∪ G
	

d) At least two events occur
This means:
    Any two or all three of the events occur

We take all intersections of pairs and the intersection of all three:
	(E∩F) ∪ (E∩G) ∪ (F∩G)

(Note: this includes the region where all three occur, which satisfies "at least two.")


e) All three events occur
	All of E, F, and G occur together.

Set-theoretic expression:
	E ∩ F ∩ G
	
	
f) None of the events occurs
	This means none of E, F, or G occur.

Set-theoretic expression:
	Ec ∩ Fc ∩ Gc


g) At most one event occurs
This includes the cases:

    No event occurs
    Exactly one of E, F, or G occurs

We break it down into disjoint regions:
    None occurs: Ec ∩ Fc ∩ Gc

    Only E occurs: E ∩ Fc ∩ Gc

    Only F occurs: F ∩ Ec ∩ Gc

    Only G occurs: G ∩ Ec ∩ Fc

Set-theoretic expression:
	(Ec∩Fc∩Gc) ∪ (E∩Fc∩Gc) ∪ (Ec∩F∩Gc) ∪ (Ec∩Fc∩G)

    No events occur
    Exactly one event occurs
    Exactly two events occur

Set-theoretic expression:
	(E∩F∩G)c



Certainly! Below are the set-theoretic expressions for each scenario using the events \( E \), \( F \), and \( G \):

### a) Only \( F \) occurs
This means \( F \) occurs, but neither \( E \) nor \( G \) occurs.
\[
F \cap E^c \cap G^c
\]

### b) Both \( E \) and \( F \) occur but not \( G \)
This is the intersection of \( E \) and \( F \) minus \( G \).
\[
E \cap F \cap G^c
\]

### c) At least one event occurs
This is the union of all three events.
\[
E \cup F \cup G
\]

### d) At least two events occur
This means any two or all three events occur. There are three possible pairs and one triple:
\[
(E \cap F) \cup (E \cap G) \cup (F \cap G)
\]

### e) All three events occur
This is the intersection of all three events.
\[
E \cap F \cap G
\]

### f) No event occurs
This is the complement of the union of all events (i.e., none of \( E \), \( F \), or \( G \) occurs).
\[
(E \cup F \cup G)^c \quad \text{or} \quad E^c \cap F^c \cap G^c
\]

### g) At most one event occurs
This means either no event occurs, or exactly one of \( E \), \( F \), or \( G \) occurs. It can be expressed as:
\[
(E^c \cap F^c \cap G^c) \cup (E \cap F^c \cap G^c) \cup (F \cap E^c \cap G^c) \cup (G \cap E^c \cap F^c)
\]
Alternatively, it's the complement of "at least two events occur":
\[
(E \cap F)^c \cap (E \cap G)^c \cap (F \cap G)^c
\]

### h) At most two events occur
This means the scenario where all three events do **not** occur simultaneously. It's the complement of "all three events occur":
\[
(E \cap F \cap G)^c
\]
Alternatively, it can be expressed as the union of all possible cases where zero, one, or two events occur.
