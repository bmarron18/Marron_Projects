## Key findings reflected in the documents

**Scope of use.** The field's central meta-review describes remote sensing as providing repetitive information on crop status throughout the season at different scales and for different actors, covering crop breeding, agricultural land use monitoring, crop yield forecasting, as well as ecosystem services in relation to soil and water resources or biodiversity loss. Sentinel-2 is the pivotal sensor: by providing freely accessible multispectral imagery with 10–20 m spatial resolution, frequent revisit times, and red-edge bands, Sentinel-2 has enabled a shift from regional assessments toward field-level and within-field yield monitoring, and its red-edge bands are also effective for monitoring nitrogen status.

**Soil chemistry (SOC, pH, CEC, N).** Recent systematic reviews find that spectral indices such as NDVI, SAVI, and EVI, along with topographic indices obtained from DEMs, were identified as the most influential inputs for SOC prediction. Due to their improved resolutions, the Sentinel-2 and Landsat 8 satellites were the main data sources. Importantly, the samples' spatial distribution and variability are more critical than the total number of samples. Accuracy is moderate: Sentinel-2 PLSR studies obtained intermediate prediction performance outcomes (R2cv and RPD greater than or equal to 0.5 and 1.4, respectively) for 4 topsoil properties found across the Versailles Plain (SOC, pH, CaCO3 and CEC), and a 2026 multi-sensor study reports PLSR-based SOC models achieved validation R² values of 0.40–0.61 with corresponding RMSEs of 0.30–0.38 across MODIS Terra, Landsat OLI, and Sentinel-2 MSI. The quantitative models exhibit satisfactory validation accuracy but limited spatial robustness across sensors in practical mapping. At global scale, SoilGrids produces maps of soil properties for the entire globe at medium spatial resolution (250 m cell size) using state-of-the-art machine learning methods... It takes as inputs soil observations from about 240 000 locations worldwide and over 400 global environmental covariates describing vegetation, terrain morphology, climate, geology and hydrology, covering soil organic carbon content, total nitrogen, coarse fragments, pH(water), cation exchange capacity, bulk density and texture fractions at six standard depths (up to 200 cm). SoilGrids includes spatial uncertainty information with the 0.05 and 0.95 quantiles for the different soil properties.

**Soil biology / bacterial–fungal ratios — an important caveat.** I found no peer-reviewed evidence that F:B ratios are measured from satellites; what exists is statistical inference. A DESIS hyperspectral study paired with eDNA found up to 50% variance in bacterial alpha diversity, and 40% of fungal alpha diversity variance, are explained by our modelling (in forests), while a cereal-cropland study associate[s] the normalized difference vegetation index with fungal microbiomes in wheat, barley, and maize using a two-step machine learning process. The first step adjusts normalized difference vegetation index values for abiotic confounders using a random forest model trained on Lucas 2018 topsoil and ERA5 climate datasets. Its authors note that studies linking remote sensing with microbial soil composition are limited when compared to that of nutrient effects. The documents therefore frame microbial metrics as "exploratory; indirect inference only," with ground methods (PLFA, qPCR) remaining the reference.

**Platforms.** Google Earth Engine is a cloud-based platform for planetary-scale geospatial analysis that brings Google's massive computational capabilities to bear on a variety of high-impact societal issues including deforestation, drought, disaster, disease, food security, water management, climate monitoring and environmental protection. The open-access Springer textbook on it is a set of 31 labs designed to take the reader from being a complete Earth Engine novice to being a quite advanced user. The second half, Applications, presents a tour of the world of Earth Engine across 24 chapters, and is licensed under a Creative Commons License (CC BY-NC-SA).
