# Analysis of the Earth Engine snippet
###########################################

The snippet builds a one-year, low-cloud median composite of Landsat 8 over a small area around Valle de Bravo, Mexico. The structure is sound, but a few details affect the scientific validity of the result. The first four items below are real problems, and the rest are refinements.

## What each part does

**1. The ROI**
```python
roi = ee.Geometry.Point([-100.088829, 19.190335])
```
- Earth Engine expects `[longitude, latitude]`, and you used that order correctly. The coordinates fall in the Valle de Bravo area of Estado de México.
- This is a server-side object: it describes the point but nothing is computed yet. Earth Engine is lazy, so almost nothing runs until you call something like `getInfo()`, `Export`, or a display method.

**2. The collection**
```python
ee.ImageCollection("LANDSAT/LC08/C02/T1_L2")
```
This is Landsat 8 Collection 2, Tier 1, Level 2 (surface reflectance plus surface temperature). T1 means the highest-quality geometric calibration.

**3. The filters**
- `filterBounds(roi)` keeps scenes whose footprint intersects the point.
- `filterDate('2022-01-01', '2022-12-31')` filters by acquisition date.
- `ee.Filter.lt('CLOUD_COVER', 10)` keeps scenes with under 10% cloud cover.

**4. The count**
`.size().getInfo()` is the first call that forces a round trip to Google's servers, so it is the first point where the script actually waits.

**5. The composite and clip**
- `.median()` computes a per-pixel, per-band median across all images, ignoring masked pixels.
- `roi.buffer(1000)` makes a circle of 1,000 m radius (about 3.14 km²), roughly 3,500 Landsat pixels at 30 m.
- `.clip()` masks everything outside that circle.

## Issues and gaps

2. **Scale factors are not applied.** This is the most important issue. Collection 2 Level 2 data is stored as scaled integers.
   - Optical bands (`SR_B1`–`SR_B7`): reflectance = DN × 0.0000275 − 0.2
   - Thermal (`ST_B10`): Kelvin = DN × 0.00341802 + 149.0

   Without this, any index you compute later (NDVI, NDWI, etc.) will be wrong, because the offset doesn't cancel in band ratios.

3. **Cloud filtering is scene-level only.** `CLOUD_COVER < 10` throws away whole scenes but does nothing about clouds, shadows, or cirrus inside the scenes you keep. A 9% cloudy scene still contributes those pixels to the median. The median is robust to some outliers, but with few images it can still leak cloud or shadow. The standard fix is to mask using the `QA_PIXEL` band before compositing.


5. **Seasonal bias.** Valle de Bravo has a pronounced rainy season (roughly June–October). A strict 10% cloud filter will likely remove many wet-season scenes, so the composite will be dominated by the dry season. That matters if you're studying vegetation or water. Check `count` and the dates of the images you keep, since Landsat 8 gives at most about 23 acquisitions per path per year.

6. **Landsat 9 is ignored.** Landsat 9 (`LANDSAT/LC09/C02/T1_L2`) has been operating since late 2021 and would roughly double your revisit frequency, which helps in cloudy regions. The two sensors are similar enough to merge for most purposes.

7. **`clip()` is optional for analysis.** It adds processing cost and isn't needed for computations such as `reduceRegion`, where you pass the geometry directly. It is useful for visualization and for exports, where `region=` also does the job.


## Improved version

```python
import ee

ee.Initialize(project="your-project-id")

roi = ee.Geometry.Point([-100.088829, 19.190335])  # lon, lat

def mask_and_scale(img):
    qa = img.select("QA_PIXEL")
    clear = (qa.bitwiseAnd(1 << 1).eq(0)    # dilated cloud
             .And(qa.bitwiseAnd(1 << 3).eq(0))  # cloud
             .And(qa.bitwiseAnd(1 << 4).eq(0))) # cloud shadow
    optical = img.select("SR_B.").multiply(0.0000275).add(-0.2)
    thermal = img.select("ST_B10").multiply(0.00341802).add(149.0)
    return (img.addBands(optical, None, True)
               .addBands(thermal, None, True)
               .updateMask(clear))

collection = (ee.ImageCollection("LANDSAT/LC08/C02/T1_L2")
    .filterBounds(roi)
    .filterDate("2022-01-01", "2023-01-01")       # end date exclusive
    .filter(ee.Filter.lt("CLOUD_COVER", 30))      # looser, since pixels are masked
    .map(mask_and_scale))

print("Images:", collection.size().getInfo())

composite = collection.median()
aoi = roi.buffer(1000)
clipped = composite.clip(aoi)

vis = {"bands": ["SR_B4", "SR_B3", "SR_B2"], "min": 0.0, "max": 0.3}
```

Once pixel-level masking is in place, the scene-level threshold can be relaxed (30% here) to keep more images and reduce the dry-season bias.
